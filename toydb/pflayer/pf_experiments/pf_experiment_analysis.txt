Workload
Page-access sequence: 0, 1, 2, 3, 0, 1, 4, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4
Logical reads per configuration: 12
Policies: LRU (0) and MRU (1)
Buffer capacities: 2, 4, 8, and 16 pages

Results
- LRU, capacity 2: 12 physical reads, 0 hits, 0.00% hit ratio.
- LRU, capacity 4: 8 physical reads, 4 hits, 33.33% hit ratio.
- LRU, capacity 8: 5 physical reads, 7 hits, 58.33% hit ratio.
- LRU, capacity 16: 5 physical reads, 7 hits, 58.33% hit ratio.
- MRU, capacity 2: 10 physical reads, 2 hits, 16.67% hit ratio.
- MRU, capacity 4: 6 physical reads, 6 hits, 50.00% hit ratio.
- MRU, capacity 8: 5 physical reads, 7 hits, 58.33% hit ratio.
- MRU, capacity 16: 5 physical reads, 7 hits, 58.33% hit ratio.

Observations
1. Increasing buffer capacity reduces physical reads for both policies until the
   workload's five distinct pages can be retained.
2. For this access sequence, MRU performs better than LRU at capacities 2 and 4.
3. At capacities 8 and 16, both policies produce the same result: 5 physical
   reads and a 58.33% hit ratio.
4. Increasing capacity from 8 to 16 does not improve this workload's measured
   results.
5. Physical writes are zero because this experiment performs page reads only;
   it does not modify pages.

Conclusion
For this specific access sequence, larger buffer capacity improves hit ratio
and reduces physical reads. MRU outperforms LRU at the two smaller capacities,
while both policies perform identically at capacities 8 and 16. These results
are workload-specific and do not prove that MRU is generally superior to LRU.

Measurement note
The hit ratio is calculated as (buffer hits / logical reads) * 100. The results
are from the standalone PF buffer-manager experiment and are independent of
the AM/B+ Tree implementation.
